On writing and thinking in public

March 8, 2026

There is something uncomfortable about publishing your thoughts before they are fully formed. Every sentence you write becomes a record of what you believed at a particular moment — a commitment you might later want to revise, or quietly forget.

And yet I think the discomfort is worth it. Writing forces a kind of clarity that private thinking rarely demands. When you have to explain an idea to someone who wasn't inside your head as it formed, you discover quickly which parts of it were solid and which were just the feeling of understanding, masquerading as the real thing.

Writing is thinking made visible. The discipline of articulation is also a discipline of thought.

The public part matters too, in a different way. The possibility that someone might read what you wrote — and disagree with it, or point out something you missed — introduces a useful kind of accountability. Thoughts that never face scrutiny tend to ossify. They become part of the furniture of your mind rather than something you continue to examine.

The fear of being wrong

The main objection to writing in public is the fear of being wrong in front of other people. This fear is not irrational. Being wrong publicly has social costs. But I think those costs are generally much smaller than they feel, and the benefits of updating your beliefs through feedback are larger than they appear.

What actually builds credibility over time is not being right from the start, but being willing to engage seriously with ideas, change your mind when the evidence warrants it, and acknowledge when you were mistaken. The people I find most intellectually trustworthy are exactly those who have visibly changed their minds about things.

Starting anyway

This is the first thing I've published here. I don't yet know what this site will become, or which of these ideas I'll still hold in five years. That uncertainty is fine. The point isn't to arrive at this already knowing — it's to think through things in writing, and see what comes out.
